# Top-500 lender churn and persistence, 2007-2025

This analysis follows lenders in the annual top-500 funded-loan ranking. The goal is to show which lenders stayed, left, newly entered, returned after disappearing, and behaved like durable incumbents versus short-lived guests.

The lender identity is based on normalized lender names, with obvious aliases combined where they matter for this file, including United Wholesale Mortgage / United Shore as UWM and Rocket Mortgage / Quicken Loans as one series. It does not attempt to fully merge every merger, acquisition, or legal-entity restructuring in the mortgage industry.

## How to read the categories

- **Remained**: appeared in the top 500 in one year and also in the next year.

- **Went away**: appeared in one year but did not appear in the next year.

- **Entrant**: appeared in the next year but not the prior year.

- **New joiner**: an entrant whose first observed top-500 appearance is that year.

- **Came back**: an entrant that had appeared before, disappeared for at least one year, and then returned.

- **Consistent**: appeared in all 19 years.

- **Old runner**: began in the 2007 top 500 and appeared in at least 10 years.

- **Flip-flopper**: moved in and out across multiple spells, or returned after a gap while having eight or fewer total appearances.

- **Guest**: appeared in exactly one year.

## What the movement says

Across the period, the average year kept **391** of the prior year top-500 lenders and lost **103**. The incoming side averaged **74** first-time new joiners and **30** comeback lenders per year. This means the top of the market is not a closed club: every year has meaningful turnover, but a durable core remains visible across most of the period.


The largest exit wave in this file is **2017 → 2018**, when **193** prior-year top-500 lenders disappeared from the next year list. The largest entrant wave is **2017 → 2018**, with **195** entrants. The largest comeback count is **2019 → 2020**, with **90** returning lenders.

## Suggested use

Use this file as a map for deeper lender-by-lender research. The year-to-year table identifies the churn years; the lifecycle tables identify names worth researching. For a UWM-focused story, the most useful next step is to cross-check UWM’s rank and funded-loan share against the lenders that exited or declined around the same windows, then separate true competitive share capture from mergers, rebrands, channel exits, and macro-rate cycles.
