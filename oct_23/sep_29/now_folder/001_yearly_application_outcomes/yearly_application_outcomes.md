# Yearly applications and outcomes

HMDA files on disk run from 2007 through 2025. An application here is a loan request the lender actually received. That is five outcomes: the loan was made, it was approved but the borrower did not take it, it was denied, the borrower withdrew it, or the file was closed as incomplete.

Loans the lender bought from someone else, and preapproval requests, are in the HMDA files but are not counted as applications in this table.

## The story

1. In 2007 the doors were crowded. Lenders across the country took in 21.4 million mortgage applications, the second-busiest year in this whole record. A lot of those people left empty-handed. About 28% were denied, and only about half walked out with a loan. Another 1.9 million were told yes and then never took the loan. The market was large, loose at the front door, and rough at the decision.

2. Then the room got quiet. By 2008 applications had fallen by a third. The strange part is what happened next. In 2009 fewer people came in, and a much larger share of them left with a loan. Denials dropped from 28% to 20%. The crowd had thinned, and the people still applying were easier to approve. For the next decade the market settled into that smaller shape: usually 11 to 15 million applications a year, about 60% becoming loans, and denials hovering near one in five.

3. There is a seam in the middle of that calm stretch. In 2018 the rules for what lenders had to report changed. The labels on the outcomes stayed the same, so the story can keep going across that year. A single jump, like denials moving from 17% in 2017 to 20% in 2018, is a muddy clue. Some of it is borrowers and lenders. Some of it is the form itself.

4. The second crowd arrived in 2020. Applications jumped to 22.7 million, past even 2007, and in 2021 they reached 23.3 million, the busiest year in these 19 years. This time the crowd was different. About 64% of applications became loans, the highest share in the series, and only about 13% were denied. Lenders made 15 million loans in 2021. If people had merely been filing extra applications and shopping around, the share that closed would have fallen. It rose. These were borrowers who already had mortgages, refinancing while rates were on the floor, and most of them qualified.

5. Then the music stopped. In 2022 applications fell to 14.3 million. In 2023 they fell to 10.0 million, and only 5.7 million loans were made, the fewest in the entire series. From the 2021 peak to that trough, applications dropped 57% and funded loans dropped 62%. The market got smaller, and a smaller piece of what remained actually closed. Denials climbed back to 20%.

6. 2024 and 2025 are the lights coming back on, slowly. Applications rose to 11.8 million. Loans made rose to 6.8 million. Denials eased from 20% to 18%. That is a real turn. It is still a long way from 2019, the last ordinary year before the boom, when 15.1 million people applied and 9.3 million loans were made. Today's market is quieter than the boom and kinder than 2007. In 2025, 58% of applications became loans and 18% were denied. Among the files that actually reached a yes or a no, about 23% were denied, compared with about 32% in 2007 and about 16% in the easy year of 2021.

7. One habit changed along the way, and it changes how the denial rate should be read. In 2007, about 14% of applications were withdrawn or left unfinished. By 2025 that was about one in five. A denial rate counted on every application looks a little gentler than a denial rate counted only on finished files, because more files now disappear before anyone says yes or no.

8. The other habit changed in the opposite direction. In 2007, nearly two million people were approved and walked away. In 2025 that number was about 400,000, roughly 4% of applications. Borrowers who get a yes now usually take it. The applications that leak out of the process are the ones that are pulled back or never finished, earlier in the story, before the lender's answer.

## Insights

1. Two cycles share one shape. A crowded, harsh year gives way to a thinner crowd that is easier to approve. That happened after 2007, and it happened again after 2021.

2. 2021 was the high point: 23.3 million applications and 15.0 million loans made. 2023 was the low point: 10.0 million applications and 5.7 million loans, the fewest in these 19 years.

3. The 2020–2021 boom was real funded lending. Volume and the share of applications that closed both rose, to about 64% made and about 13% denied.

4. 2024 and 2025 are a modest recovery. Applications went from 10.0 million to 11.8 million, loans made from 5.7 million to 6.8 million, and denials from 20% to 18%. That is still short of 2019.

5. 2025 is quieter than the boom and kinder than 2007. About 58% of applications became loans and 18% were denied. In 2007 those shares were about 49% and 28%.

6. About one in five applications now ends with no yes and no no, because the borrower withdrew or the file was never finished. In 2007 that share was about 14%.

7. Approvals that the borrower walks away from have become rare: 1.9 million in 2007, and about 0.4 million in 2025.

8. The 2018 reporting-rule change means a one-year move in the denial share, such as 17% in 2017 to 20% in 2018, should be read gently. The outcome labels stayed the same. The set of loans that had to be reported did not.
