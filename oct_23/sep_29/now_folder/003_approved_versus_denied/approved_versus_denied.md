# Approved versus denied

Every application lands in one of two places. The lender approved it, or it ended on the denied side. The smaller cases sit underneath those two, so the list reads in a straight line.

Same applications as the yearly outcomes note: a request the lender received, from 2007 through 2025. Loans the lender bought, and preapproval requests, stay out.

```
Application
├── Approved
│   ├── Loan made
│   └── Approved, not taken
└── Denied side
    ├── Lender said no
    ├── Borrower withdrew
    └── Incomplete file
```

**Approved** means the lender said yes. **Loan made** means the borrower took it and the loan closed. **Approved, not taken** means the lender said yes and the borrower walked away.

**Denied side** means the application did not end in an approval. Three things sit here. **Lender said no** is HMDA's own denial. **Borrower withdrew** means the applicant pulled the file before a decision. **Incomplete file** means the lender closed it for missing information. Those last two are grouped here so the two sides add up to every application. In the HMDA rulebook, only "lender said no" is a denial.

## Insights

1. In 2025, about 62 of every 100 applications were approved and about 38 ended on the denied side. In 2007 those shares were 58 and 42. The two doors moved, and they did not flip.

2. The approved share rose to 67% in 2021, the high point, and then settled back near 62% by 2025. The denied side was 33% in the boom and 38% in 2025.

3. Once a lender says yes, the loan usually closes. In 2025, 94% of approvals became a loan and 6% were approved but not taken. In 2007, 16% of approvals were walked away from. That subtype has become a small branch under approved.

4. The denied side used to be mostly a lender saying no. In 2007, 66% of that side was a denial, 26% was a withdrawal, and 8% was an incomplete file. In 2025 those shares are 47%, 38%, and 15%.

5. In 2020 and 2021 the largest piece of the denied side was the borrower withdrawing, ahead of the lender's no. A lot of what looks like "not approved" in the boom years is an applicant leaving, often while shopping another offer.

6. Incomplete files are the smallest branch, and they have grown. They were 3.4% of all applications in 2007 and 5.9% in 2025. Inside the denied side they went from 8% to 15%.

7. Read the denied side as three different endings. A lender's no is a credit decision. A withdrawal is the borrower leaving. An incomplete file is a form that never got finished. Adding them together shows how many applications failed to become an approval. Keeping them separate shows why.
